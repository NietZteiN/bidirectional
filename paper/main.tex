\begin{abstract}
Many NLP tasks come in pairs whose training instances are the same data read in two directions:
obfuscation and deobfuscation, translation each way, text-to-SQL and SQL-to-text, data-to-text
and text-to-data. Fine-tuning on one direction destroys the ability the model already had in the
other. We characterise this across \NUM{n-domains} paired domains and \NUM{n-families} model
families, and show three things about it. The loss is disproportionate: the inverse direction
falls to \NUM{abstract-reverse-after} while held-out general ability moves \NUM{abstract-general-cost}.
It is cheap to prevent: replacing \NUM{abstract-knee} of the forward pairs with their own
reversal restores \NUM{abstract-restored} of the lost capability, at matched instances, sequence
tokens and optimizer steps. And what is lost is suppressed rather than erased --- recoverable by
prompting, by scaling the adapter, by a few reversed examples, and by a closed-form filter on the
weight update that uses no data at all. Invertibility bounds the repair, and separates two things
usually run together: an inverse that is undetermined cannot be restored at any dose, while one
that is merely hard to find can be. A consequence follows for attribution: any method credited
with restoring an inverse capability on paired data must be compared against a baseline supplying
the same directional content and none of the method.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Paired data carries a direction. Obfuscating a program rewrites it so that it behaves identically
but is hard to read; a model can be trained to perform that rewrite, or to undo it. The two tasks
use the same pairs of programs, and training the second only requires swapping which half of each
pair is the input. Compilation and decompilation, minification and beautification, translation
between two languages, and the two directions of semantic parsing all work this way.

% The hook: the data that would have prevented the failure was already in hand.
This structure is what makes the failure we study both possible and avoidable. Fine-tuning a
model on the forward direction of such a pair degrades its ability in the reverse direction ---
an ability the base model already had. \citet{nikiema2025contrastive} name this
\emph{cognitive specialization} and document it for code obfuscation;
\citet{zhu2024finetuning} report the same shape in machine translation, where fine-tuning with
English on the target side damages translation into other languages. We take the phenomenon as
established in those settings and ask what is true of it in general.

\paragraph{The loss is disproportionate, which is why it is easy to miss.}
\NUM{intro-disproportion-sentence}

\paragraph{It is cheap to prevent.}
\NUM{intro-dose-sentence}

\paragraph{Invertibility bounds the repair, and it is two properties, not one.}
An inverse can be \emph{undetermined} --- the information needed is not present in the input, and
no quantity of reverse data can supply it --- or \emph{determined but hard to find}, where the
information is there and search is the obstacle. These predict different things about a cure, and
\S\ref{sec:invertibility} separates them with a synthetic ladder that varies determinability
directly and a domain that holds determinability fixed while varying search difficulty.

\paragraph{What is lost is suppressed, not erased.}
\NUM{intro-mechanism-sentence}

\paragraph{A general test follows, and it needs no new data.}
For any method that adds instances to paired training data, train the arm that supplies the same
directional content and none of the method. This is more than advice because the ablation arm is
also the remedy: running it pays for itself whichever way the comparison falls. \S\ref{sec:attrib}
applies it to \NUM{n-attrib-methods} published objectives.

\section{Directional collapse, defined}
\label{sec:defined}

A \emph{paired task} is a pair of datasets $(X, Y)$ and $(Y, X)$ over the same instances, where
the reverse training set is obtained from the forward one by exchanging input and target. We call
$X \to Y$ the \emph{forward} direction and $Y \to X$ the \emph{reverse}. \emph{Directional
collapse} is a fall in reverse-direction success after fine-tuning on forward-direction data
alone, measured against the untouched model.

Every domain has a strict per-instance criterion with an automatic check
(\S\ref{sec:setup}). Two failure modes are tracked separately and never counted as success:

\begin{description}
\item[Echo] the output reproduces its own input. This is not hygiene. In JSON-to-YAML
  conversion, JSON is valid YAML, so a model that copies its input \emph{parses} and
  \emph{compares structurally equal} to the target; without the echo guard the task is not a
  task. Echo rates rose under forward-only training in earlier work, so a criterion that admits
  them measures the criterion rather than the capability.
\item[Off-target] the output is in the wrong language, format, or notation.
\end{description}

Thresholds for continuous criteria are fixed from the \emph{untouched} model's own score
distribution before any fine-tuned model is scored, and are recorded with the date and quantile
that produced them.

\paragraph{Arms and budget.}
The forward-only baseline (\textsc{sft}) trains on forward pairs. Each \textsc{mix}$k$ arm
replaces $k\%$ of those pairs with their own reversal, partitioned by pair identity so that no
pair is seen in both directions. Because the reversal \emph{replaces} rather than adds, instance
count, sequence tokens and optimizer steps are matched to \textsc{sft} at every dose, and the
only quantity varying along the ladder is the share of pairs seen backwards.
\citet{golovneva2024reversetraining} distinguish data-matched from compute-matched reverse
training; replacement is matched on both simultaneously, which is what licenses the word
``free'' in \S\ref{sec:dose} --- scoped, there and throughout, to training budget.

Two controls separate directional loss from its alternatives. \textsc{replay} replaces the same
share with generic instruction data, so \textsc{replay}~$-$~\textsc{sft} is ordinary forgetting
and \textsc{mix}~$-$~\textsc{replay} is what direction specifically buys. \textsc{rev} trains on
reverse pairs only and is the ceiling every reverse number is read against; where \textsc{rev} is
near zero the direction is not learnable in that cell and no null there is interpretable.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Four lines of work meet here. Each has a sentence saying what separates it from directional
collapse, because in three of the four the distinction is the contribution.

\paragraph{The phenomenon, in single domains.}
\citet{nikiema2025contrastive} name \emph{cognitive specialization}: fine-tuning on a forward
code transformation not only fails to produce the reverse but degrades the bidirectional
reasoning a base model already had. \citet{zhu2024finetuning} report the same shape in machine
translation, where fine-tuning on a single direction generalizes to others \emph{except} when
English is on the target side, which causes task misinterpretation. We take the phenomenon as
established in those settings. What is open is whether it is general, what predicts the size of
the loss, how little reversed data prevents it, and whether what is lost is gone.
% The paper's own premise needs base models to be able to do both directions. DexBench is
% independent, contemporaneous evidence for that in the execution domain, and its benchmark is
% the eval set of one of our cells.
\citet{hasanov2026pathnottaken} evaluate 13 models on the duality of program-execution reasoning
and find backward performance far below forward throughout, which is the premise this paper
depends on: the capability is there to be destroyed.

\paragraph{The reversal curse: a capability never acquired.}
\citet{berglund2024reversal} fine-tune on ``$A$ is $B$'' and find the reverse is not learned;
\citet{golovneva2024reversetraining} cure it at the data level with reverse training, evaluated
both data-matched and compute-matched. \textbf{The distinction is not rhetorical here, it is an
experiment.} That literature describes a capability the model never had. Ours had it, and lost
it. \S\ref{sec:mechanism} measures relearning cost from a collapsed model against a
\emph{never-had control} --- an invented transformation no pretraining corpus contains --- and
the two curves are what separate the cases. A second difference is budget: reverse training
doubles the token count, while replacing forward pairs with their own reversal holds instances,
sequence tokens and optimizer steps fixed at once.

\paragraph{Forgetting: diffuse loss, versus targeted and total.}
Fine-tuning degrades capabilities its data never threatened, across model scales
\citep{luo2023forgetting}, and LoRA forgets less than full fine-tuning
\citep{biderman2024lora} --- which makes the magnitudes we report on LoRA conservative, and is
why \S\ref{sec:setup} includes full fine-tuning arms. The difference from directional collapse
is shape: forgetting is measured as a spread-out decline across unrelated benchmarks, where here
the inverse direction goes to near zero while general probes move a few points.
% Scialom is the closest antecedent to the dose result and the honest way to use it is as the
% source of a prediction our own control tests, not as prior art to be distinguished away.
\citet{scialom2022rehearsal} show that a memory buffer of roughly 1\,\% of prior data preserves
almost all earlier-task performance across eight sequential tasks. That is the same shape as our
dose result and we do not claim the shape as new. Two things differ, and the second is testable:
their buffer replays genuine earlier-task examples and \emph{adds} to the training budget, where
a reversed pair is constructed for free and \emph{replaces}; and if directional collapse were
ordinary forgetting, their result predicts that generic rehearsal at the same share should
prevent it. \textsc{replay} is that arm.

\paragraph{Suppression rather than removal, and skewed task inference.}
That fine-tuning modulates rather than replaces is already well supported outside this setting,
and \S\ref{sec:mechanism} does not claim it as new. Fine-tuning rarely alters underlying
capabilities and learns a minimal ``wrapper'' over them \citep{jain2024wrapper}; DPO does not
remove the ability to produce toxic text but bypasses it with an offset distributed across
layers \citep{lee2024dpo} --- a distribution that is itself a prediction for our layer
ablation; and damaged capabilities can be recovered post hoc by filtering the weight update
\citep{spectral2026unforgetting}. Closest of all is \citet{kotha2024forgetting}, who argue that
models implicitly infer the task of a prompt and that fine-tuning \emph{skews that inference}
toward the fine-tuning distribution, recovering capability by making the prompt look farther
from it. Our hypothesis is their mechanism with direction as the inferred task: forward-only
training leaves the model applying the trained mapping whichever direction is asked for.
What \S\ref{sec:mechanism} adds is the directional case, a never-had control that makes
erased-versus-suppressed a measurement rather than an inference, and four instruments that can
disagree. \citet{latentgen2026illusion} add a caution we
take seriously: objectives that repair reversal behaviour need not build a single
direction-agnostic representation, and their probes are consistent with forward and reverse
stored as distinct entries. That is why \S\ref{sec:mechanism} uses four instruments rather than
one, and why both directions share a single system prompt --- if directions can be stored
separately while behaviour looks bidirectional, two personas would let disjoint circuits
masquerade as a unified one.

\paragraph{Direction as a confound in attribution.}
The methodological consequence has a literature of its own: gains attributed to an objective
often belong to the data mixture \citep{longpre2023flan}, and reporting practice makes such
claims hard to check \citep{lipton2018troubling}. \citet{chen2025revthink} is the sharpest
current instance, and a useful one because nothing about it is careless. RevThink augments each
example with a teacher-generated backward question and backward reasoning and improves
\emph{forward} accuracy by 13.53\,\% across 12 datasets. Its objective is
$\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{3n}\sum_i [\ell(\text{forward}) + \ell(\text{backward question}) +
\ell(\text{backward reasoning})]$, a sum of cross-entropy terms over a union of three formats
--- which is what supervised fine-tuning on that union is. Its baselines differ from it in data
direction as well as in method. When an objective has that form, the objective and the data
mixture are not separable by any experiment the paper contains. That is the general problem
being structural rather than accidental, and it is why the test in \S\ref{sec:intro} is worth
stating rather than assuming.

\section{Conclusion}
For any method that adds instances to paired training data, train the arm that supplies the same
directional content and none of the method.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Where the repair does not reach.} \NUM{lim-invertibility}

\paragraph{Scale.} The full arm set runs at 1--4B with three seeds; only core arms run at
8--12B with two. \NUM{lim-scale}

\paragraph{LoRA.} Every arm is trained with LoRA, which maintains out-of-domain performance
better than full fine-tuning \citep{biderman2024lora}; the collapse magnitudes we report are
therefore conservative, and the full fine-tuning arms in \S\ref{sec:setup} bound the difference.
\NUM{lim-lora}

\paragraph{Resolution of the dose ladder.} Adjacent rungs differ by a couple of points, which
our evaluation sets cannot separate reliably. We therefore report the knee as a bound rather
than a point estimate. \NUM{lim-knee}

\paragraph{Criteria with a model in them.} Two directions are scored by round-tripping through a
frozen model rather than by execution. Each reports that model's own accuracy on the references
as the criterion's ceiling. \NUM{lim-frozen-scorers}

\paragraph{Languages and domains.} The code cell is Python only; the translation cells cover two
language pairs, both with English on one side. \NUM{lim-coverage}
